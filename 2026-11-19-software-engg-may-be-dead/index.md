_“All men [or women] want, not something to do with, but something to do, or rather something to be.”_
~ Walden by Henry David Thoreau

> Context: AI is a huge umbrella. I mostly mean LLMs in the scope of this blog post.

I have been wanting to share my thoughts about AI for quite a while now. However, it’s not as simple as just writing about another technology. Is it? I think most people struggle with the complexities of their feelings and thoughts about AI, especially when doing something creative. Of course, I am no exception. There’s a dilemma I struggle with. On one hand, I don’t like the outputs of AI, on the other I understand that it’s quite a capable tool.

## “AI is going to replace software engineers”

This is a familiar headline since the past few years. Most of me laughs at that. But *a part of me* wonders about its truth. Nobody can see the future, so, what if the headline is true? Sorry, I digress. This post is not about the future, it’s about the present. AI is already a capable programmer. It has been, since yesterday. Now what?

## Should I stop writing code?
For all of the previous drafts of this post, I have been trying to figure out the answer to that question. I have a lot of thoughts, but there’s no coherence in my thinking. **I have been writing to bring clarity to my understanding about AI.** (yes, with a pen, on a paper)

Read the previous sentence again. No, really, do it. Rest of the text can wait.

Many people have told me that AI could have written this for me, in matter of minutes, if not seconds. Given the fact that I have a few drafts, it could do even better. It’ll spit out one or more coherent essay(s) from my haphazard sentences. I could simply hit “Publish” and move on to the next thing.

Sorry to ask again, but please go and read the last bold sentence again. AI may able to untangle my sentences, but not my thoughts to bring better understanding. It’s in the act of writing that I grow. To me, the answer to whether I should stop writing code or not is quite clear: NO. Without writing code, I cannot get any closer to being better.

## Do we simply write code?
It’s no secret that software engineers don’t just write code. We understand problems, that is most of the job. We understand trade-offs of multiple possible solutions, and figure out how to balance them for the problem proposed. Part of that understanding comes from discussions with people: clients, business analysts, designers, product owners etc. Another part comes from expertise.

Expertise comes from knowledge, experience, and context, among many other things. Admittedly, a human brain cannot contain everything ever published about computing. So, arguably, knowledge is the only thing where an AI system scores more than me. An LLM’s “context” is a misnomer, among many other things in tech. But that discussion is for another day.

Lastly, experience is something that an AI doesn’t have. More appropriately, cannot have. Experience is what enables us to learn and understand. Just like we write in English (or another human language) to being clarity to our thoughts; so do software developers write code, among other things, to understand the problem better, even understand computing and context better. 

Any software developer who has done any prototyping, already knows what I mean. Clearly, writing code does make one a better software developer, build better products, better their understanding of those products. Without writing code, a developer would miss-understand many details. But sure, one can “move fast and break things” with AI.

## What am I going to do?
I have been in the web development for almost 8 years now. Although, I started my career with systems programming languages in gaming 13 years ago, I am quite rusty on that front. With the advent of AI, I have been reminded that web-dev is basically built on layers on layers of abstraction. 

While web-dev pays the bills, I want to build software with longevity in mind. The mantra of “move fast and break things” is, probably, good to raise money and make those Bs. However, I studied engineering to build things, not to break them.

I enjoy programming. Why would I delegate something I enjoy? Writing/reading code is fun for me, so much so that sometimes it even brings a flow state. Why would I ask an AI system to do it?

I am jumping into learning a new systems programming language. A language which took a stance against AI, and for longevity. Take a guess, if you wish, the name of the language is at the end of the post. (To keep spoilers away :D)

## Maybe I am stupid.
No matter what we think logically, a handmade sweater is always going to feel warmer than a machine-made one. As non-logical, or even illogical, to some it may be. I am deciding to write software by-hand. It does not mean “No AI”. I will be using AI to do the things that help me write code eg. understand errors, search the docs etc. but not to auto-complete my Zig code.
